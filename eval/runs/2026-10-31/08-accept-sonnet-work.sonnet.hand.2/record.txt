### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continuing costs 1.29 USD against 2.64 USD, half the price. The session already holds the diff and the tests in context, there is no compaction risk (420,000 against 970,000), and a handoff is work. Against that, the session that wrote the change is the author, so its acceptance shares the author's blind spots; that is what row 4 and guardrail 6 forbid, and no cost margin overrides a quality rule.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured (300,000). 30 percent off changes only the continue cost, which is already the cheaper side. No effect on a verdict set by class.
- T: 15 is an estimate. Doubled to 30 is a stress test (below). Under 10 turns, guardrail 1 sends the work to an Opus sub-agent, not to continue, so the class still decides.
- F and O: estimates. At high effort, O may run higher than 3,000. With O at 5,000, fresh output rises to 1.50 and continue output to 0.75; the order does not change.
- B and R: B is measured. R is an estimate from the situation. If R were 60,000, fresh rises by about 0.15 plus read and write terms, still the dearer side, the class decision unchanged.
- M (review pauses): none expected, so M = 0 (default, unmeasured). One miss would add about 0.04 (continue, 360,000 * 2.4/M = 0.86 per miss at sonnet prices; fresh 176,450 * 4.8/M = 0.85). Both sides rise by a similar amount; no change in verdict.
- Task type: acceptance of a refactor and tests. If the change touched auth, payments or a public interface, effort would still be high. Not a release or a merge, so no xhigh.
- Prices: table 1 day old, no long-context tier on sonnet or opus. Applied correctly.
- Unrecorded context: none, per the situation. If some existed, fresh would pay L turns and writing it into H; it would not change the class verdict.
