### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: Continue. The situation says everything the next stage needs is already on disk, so the handoff adds no knowledge, only cost and about 15 minutes of handoff and read time. A fresh session pays B, which is an unmeasured default of 25,000 tokens, and must re-read R. The margin is 0.15 USD on a 1.26 USD stage. A session that is already warm avoids the cold start on a 30-turn refactor, and the verdict is within a few percent of tied on a cost figure built from defaults.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: estimated by the characters rule, not measured. 30 percent low (80,500) moves the cost gap to 3.5 percent, inside the band.
- T: single value, no range. Doubled to 60, fresh leads by 12.8 percent. Halved to 15, the gap is 6.5 percent.
- F and O: defaults. O at 3,000 (high effort or heavier writing) leaves a 7.8 percent gap.
- B and R: B is an unmeasured default. At 40,000, the gap is 4.8 percent. R omits project instruction files, which the situation does not list.
- M (review pauses): none expected. Two misses would add about 0.9 USD to continue and 0.5 to fresh, which only widens the gap.
- Task type: a refactor could hide a design choice if it changes a public interface. That would trigger guardrail 2 only if design-heavy and crossing. Stated as not design-heavy.
- Prices: table 1 day old; sonnet row complete; no long-context tier on sonnet; cache_write derived as 1.25 times input per the table note.
- Unrecorded context: claimed empty by the situation. If 5 turns of rediscovery were needed (L = 5), fresh rises to 1.45 and continue is cheaper by 2.8 percent, inside the band.
