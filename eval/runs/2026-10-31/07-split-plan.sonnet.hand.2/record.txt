### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: plain fresh at Opus, with no split. The acceptance session adds 15 turns and 1.90 USD, and the Sonnet-class run could miss a cross-module interaction the design note did not spell out, which an Opus run would catch while it works. Continuing is the other contrary case: this session holds the design and writes no handoff, so there is no cold start and no brief to get wrong.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured. If 30 percent lower (245,000), cost_continue is 11.03 USD, still 96 percent above fresh. Does not move the verdict.
- T: could double to 100. It could also be 25 if the tests pass sooner. The doubled case is recomputed below.
- F and O: defaults, not measured. A larger O raises every side, and the Fable side most (50 USD per million output against 20 and 10).
- B and R: B measured. R is the situation's figure; the modules may be larger than 30,000, which raises the fresh sides only slightly.
- M (review pauses): 0 because none are expected. Each miss costs more on the larger continuing context, so any pause favours fresh and split further.
- Task type: the largest risk is that the stage hides a design choice. The design note states every contract, so it is row 2 work. This is the reason the acceptance piece exists.
- Prices: 1 day old, no long-context tier on these rows.
- Unrecorded context: none named. If anything were unrecorded it would be written into the brief (output cost) or priced as L.
