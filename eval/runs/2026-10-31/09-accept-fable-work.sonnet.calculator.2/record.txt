### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continue costs 3.59 USD against 5.07 USD, saves 1.48 USD (41 percent), avoids 5 minutes of reading and the operator's click, and C0 150,000 is 15 percent of the window with no compaction in sight. The session also already holds the model's "why", so its review would start warm. Against that: the warm context is what makes it a poor acceptor, because it shares the author's blind spots, and it cannot set its own effort to xhigh.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured by the usage tool. At 30 percent off (105,000 to 195,000), continue stays cheaper on cost; break-even C0 would need to be near 86,450 + 30,000 + 1.581/(12 x ... ) so no plausible shift flips the cost side.
- T: 12 is an estimate; doubled to 24 with M 2 the cost sides come within 4.1 percent (see recompute). A stress test only.
- F and O: estimates at high effort. At xhigh, O would be larger (reasoning is billed as output), so both costs rise and the fresh side gains slightly because the growth terms are equal. Neither moves the guardrail result.
- B and R: B measured. R is an estimate from the situation; if the fresh session also reads the persona and project files, R is larger and fresh costs more by 12.5 USD per million per added token, still small.
- M (review pauses): none expected, so 0. Each miss adds more to the larger continue context, which favours fresh.
- Task type: acceptance is row 4 and is clearly design-heavy (cost model). Not mechanical.
- Prices: table 1 day old, no long-context tier on the fable row. The 2026-10-29 fetch is cited in the table.
- Unrecorded context: none, per the situation. If any exists, L rises and fresh costs more.
