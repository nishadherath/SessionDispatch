### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: fresh is 0.16 USD (27.4 percent) cheaper, because 650,000 tokens of context is read from cache on every turn and a fresh start at 119,450 average context is far smaller. Everything the next stage needs is already on disk, so nothing is lost, and the break-even T* of 5.1 turns is below the 8 turns planned. A handoff of 80 lines is cheap. Against it: guardrail 1 is a hard stop, and the saving is under 20 cents against the work of writing and committing a handoff.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured. If 30 percent lower (455,000), cost_continue is about 0.53 USD against 0.58 fresh, so continue would also win on cost.
- T: estimated at 8. If it doubles to 16, fresh wins on cost and guardrail 1 no longer applies. A claimed range was not given.
- F and O: defaults, unmeasured, at medium effort. They move both sides almost equally.
- B and R: B measured; R stated. Nothing left out was named. Any larger R only raises the fresh cost.
- M (review pauses): none expected. One miss would add about 1.7 USD to continue (668,000 x 2.4e-6 = 1.6) against 0.29 for fresh, which favours fresh, but none is expected.
- Task type: a refactor within one module, test suite as oracle; no design choice is hidden in the situation.
- Prices: table 1 day old; sonnet has no long-context tier.
- Unrecorded context: none, so L is 0.
