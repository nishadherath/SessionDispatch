### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continue. The session already holds the design, and the brief is a cost (10 minutes) plus a cold start. Fresh costs the fable session's handoff and the operator's clicks. If the brief cannot fully carry the contract, the Sonnet run may stall and the split falls back to fresh anyway. Against that: the continue side at fable prices is 12.34 USD against 4.77 USD, a 2.6x gap that no plausible value of T closes.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured. If 30 percent off (245,000), continue falls further and the gap to fresh narrows, but cache reads at fable's 0.25 stay dearer than any other plan's. Continue would still be dearer than the split.
- T: estimated at 50 from the plan, no per-turn measurement. Halved or doubled tested below.
- F and O (high effort): defaults. A read-heavy stage (F 5,000 to 8,000) raises all sides; the split's relative advantage stays.
- B and R: B measured. R is the situation's figure; the brief may be a little larger than the design note if the 14 tests need quoting. Small effect.
- M (review pauses): none expected, so 0. With M = 3 on both sides the continue/fresh/split gap widens (continue 29.34, fresh 8.92, split 6.42 USD).
- Task type: the stage is labelled cross-module (row 3). Whether it is "really" row 2 depends on the brief; the split depends on that. If the brief cannot carry the contract and the tests, the split is only a figure, and the plan is fresh at opus.
- Prices: table 1 day old; no long-context tier on these rows. Cache-write for Anthropic rows is derived (1.25 x input) per the table's own note, not read from the page.
- Unrecorded context: none, per the situation. If something is hidden, L > 0 would add to fresh and split alike.

Verdict holds: yes, fragile: the split-over-fresh choice flips at T of 25 or fewer turns (the order between continue and the other plans does not flip at any T tested). T = 25 is a stress value for a plan stated at 50, not a range the situation claims, so the draft is kept.
