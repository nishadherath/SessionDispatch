### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: Continue keeps the spec and the reasons behind the design in a session that holds them now, and it has not compacted. A fresh session costs a handoff, a 50,000-token re-read and a colder start, all to save 0.29 USD on a 12-turn stage. The margin is small in dollars, and a session that runs the test suite at its own class avoids the risk of a handoff losing a detail. Against the verdict, the stage is a plain test suite whose oracle is in the spec, so the cheaper class does the work well.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured. At 30 percent off in either direction the verdict holds (C0 at 88,000 gives margin 21 percent; C0 at 143,000 widens it, since C0 does not enter C_fresh).
- T: a single estimate. At T = 24 the margin is 1.06 USD (53.9 percent). At T = 6 continue is cheaper, and the verdict flips.
- F and O: F is the default. O is the largest sensitivity. One O on both sides at 1,500 (the high-effort default) gives continue by quality, margin 6.3 percent. The flip point is about 1,700 tokens per turn. The formula uses one O for both sides, but Sonnet at medium is expected to write less than Opus at high. With Opus at 1,500 and Sonnet at 1,000, fresh still wins by about 17 percent (hand computation).
- B and R: B measured. R is the situation's 50,000. At R = 80,000 margin is 14.5 percent, and fresh still wins.
- M (review pauses): none expected. Each miss adds about 0.70 USD on the continue side and 0.41 USD on the fresh side, so misses favour fresh.
- Task type: the spec states the expected outputs, so the work is row 2. If writing those outputs needed design choices the stage would be row 3, and the target would be Opus.
- Prices: table 1 day old, both rows complete, no tier applies.
- Unrecorded context: the situation says none. Each rediscovery turn costs about 0.06 USD on the fresh side. At 3 or more rediscovery turns the margin falls under 10 percent and continue wins by quality.
