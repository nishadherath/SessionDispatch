### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: go fresh. A fresh session starts with a clean, smaller Messages history (96,450 tokens against 90,000 in the current session is not smaller, but it carries only the handoff and the edit list). On cost it is only 0.03 USD dearer, a 11.5 percent margin that sits just over the 10 percent line, so a small shift in any input could put the two within the band. Inside the band the quality terms decide, and a fresh start loses nothing here because everything is on disk.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured (90,000). If 30 percent higher (117,000) every continue turn pays the tier, which raises continue's cost; if 30 percent lower (63,000) continue pays the tier for fewer turns. Fresh does not depend on C0. Continue gets dearer with a higher C0, and fresh would then lead on cost. C0 is a measurement, so this is a stress test only.
- T: a plan estimate of 20 with no range. If it doubles to 40 the calculator gives continue 0.60 and fresh 0.64, margin 5.8 percent, so the verdict is continue by quality (band). Still continue.
- F and O: defaults, unmeasured, set for medium effort. Larger values grow both contexts at the same rate and raise both costs; the gap stays near the same size.
- B and R: B measured, R stated. If R were larger, fresh gets dearer and continue is favoured further.
- M (review pauses): no pauses expected, M = 0, unmeasured. With M = 2 the calculator gives continue 0.39 and fresh 0.43, margin 10.2 percent, continue by cost.
- Task type: row 1 holds. The edits are a reviewed list and the check script is the oracle; no hidden design choice. If the list turns out ambiguous, that is a scope change for the operator, not an input of this record.
- Prices: table 1 day old. The long-context tier is applied turn by turn for both sides. A model without the tier is not under consideration, since the work belongs to haiku class.
- Unrecorded context: none. If the claim were wrong, L above 0 would raise fresh's cost further.
