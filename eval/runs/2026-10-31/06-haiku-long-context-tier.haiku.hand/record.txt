### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: Fresh is the cheaper arm if the re-read set is smaller than stated. Its per-turn reading is always higher than the continue reading by B + R - C0 = 6,450 tokens, so fresh wins only if that gap closes. A fresh session also drops the 3,550 tokens of Messages that this stage does not need. A reviewed list with a check script needs little of the conversation, so the cold start may cost less in practice than the formula counts.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:
- C0: measured. At 30 percent above (117,000) the gap falls to 5.1 percent, inside 10 percent, so the quality rule gives continue. At 30 percent below, continue is cheaper still. The verdict flips only near 130,500 (about 45 percent above).
- T: a doubled T (40 turns) gives continue about 0.62 USD and fresh about 0.65 USD, a gap near 4 percent. Continue still wins under the quality rule. Approximate, all-long-tier.
- F and O: both sides carry G, so both rise with it. The 6,450-token reading gap is fixed by B + R - C0, so the verdict does not depend on G. Not recomputed in full.
- B and R: R at 30 percent less (7,000) gives a gap of 10.3 percent, still on cost. Below about 6,800 tokens of R the gap falls under 10 percent and the quality rule gives continue. The verdict holds either way.
- M (review pauses): none expected. Each miss costs more on the fresh side (about 0.0037 USD more per miss at the average context), so misses widen the continue lead.
- Task type: the list is reviewed, so the stage is mechanical. If an edit turns out to need judgement, that edit should go to a Sonnet piece on its own, not change this verdict.
- Prices: the table is 1 day old. Without the long-context tier, continue is 0.0528 and fresh 0.0674, a gap of 27.7 percent. The tier narrows the margin in percentage terms, and the verdict holds either way.
- Unrecorded context: the list is empty. Any unrecorded item would add to the fresh side (L turns at the fresh average), so it widens the continue lead.
