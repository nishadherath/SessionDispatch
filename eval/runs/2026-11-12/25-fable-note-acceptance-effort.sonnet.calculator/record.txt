### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continuing costs 3.45 USD against 5.33 USD, saves 1.88 USD and needs no 80-line handoff, no 5-minute cold start and no operator click. The session already holds the cost model's reasoning, so it would re-derive faster. T* is 292.6 turns, so by cost alone fresh does not pay at any plausible T. The reply to this is the guardrail: an author that checks its own work shares its blind spots, and guardrail 6 puts a cost rule below a quality rule.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured at 150,000. If 30 percent higher (195,000) T* falls to 125.0 turns; still far above T.
- T: 12 is a plan estimate. At double (24) continue is 7.35 USD and fresh 8.93 USD (with C0 at 195,000), T 24 is still under T* 125.0.
- F and O (and the effort O assumes): estimated at 5,000 and 3,000 at high. At O 1,500 (the model note's high default) continue is 2.42 USD and fresh 4.30 USD. Neither flips the cost draft.
- B and R: B measured. R of 30,000 is stated; if the fresh session reads more, fresh costs 16 USD per million extra tokens read, i.e. 0.016 USD per 1,000 tokens, which does not change the guardrail 6 outcome.
- M (review pauses): none expected, ttl 1h, effort unchanged, no model switch. One miss adds about 3.2 USD to the continue side (C_cont 198,000 at 15.8 USD per million) and about 2.6 USD to fresh (C_fresh 164,450); it favours fresh slightly and does not change the final verdict.
- Task type: row 4 is clear. The hidden risk is treating this as row 5 (the cost model is a whole-project assessment); both rows name Fable at high, so the class and effort do not change.
- Prices: table 1 day old; the fable-5-2 1-hour write is 16 USD per million (2 times input). Priced at 5-minute writes (10 USD) fresh would be cheaper in absolute terms, with the same ordering of the sides.
- Unrecorded context: the list is empty per the situation. If it were not, each item would be written into the handoff or priced as L turns.
