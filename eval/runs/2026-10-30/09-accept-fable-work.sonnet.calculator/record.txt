### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continue costs 3.59 USD against 5.07 USD for fresh, 41.2 percent less, and this session already holds the cost model's context, so there is no handoff to write and no cold start. T* is 188.5 turns, far past 12. A fresh session adds about 15 minutes of handoff time. These are the best case for continue. They lose only to guardrail 6: an author reviewing its own work shares its own blind spots, which is the reason the guardrail exists.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured. At 30 percent off (105,000 or 195,000) T* stays above 100 turns, so cost does not flip.
- T: if it doubles to 24, see the recomputation; continue stays cheaper.
- F and O (and the effort O assumes): both estimates; O assumes high effort, and xhigh raises reasoning, so O may be larger. Larger O raises both sides by the same T*O*p_out and does not change the sign.
- B and R: B measured. R is the situation's 30,000; if the handoff pulls in more files R grows and fresh gets dearer, but the guardrail decides anyway.
- M (review pauses): 0, none expected. One miss on each side would add 0.198 * 12.5/0.25 style costs: continue M*198,000*(12.5-0.25)/1e6 = 2.43 USD, fresh M*164,450*12.25/1e6 = 2.01 USD, which favours fresh slightly, not continue.
- Task type: row 4 confirmed, and the author class (fable) comes from the situation, not a guess. The stage is design-heavy but does not cross a compaction.
- Prices: table is 1 day old, no long-context tier on the fable row, none applied.
- Unrecorded context: none per the situation, so L = 0.
