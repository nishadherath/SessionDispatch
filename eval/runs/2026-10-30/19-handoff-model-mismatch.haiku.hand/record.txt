### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continue. On cost alone, continuing costs 0.43 USD against 1.55 USD for fresh, 259 percent cheaper. The session holds 95,000 tokens, well under K, has nothing unrecorded, and a fresh session pays a 101,450-token baseline and re-read before it does any work. A handoff is itself work, and a refactor with a test oracle is mostly mechanical. The matrix puts a module refactor at Sonnet, though, and step 2 rule 1 puts a higher target class ahead of cost. This argument does not change the verdict.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured at 95,000. If it were 30 percent high (123,500), the continuing side would pay the long-context tier from turn 1. Fresh still wins by class.
- T: 30. If it doubled to 60, see the recompute below. Continue stays cheaper, and fresh still wins by class.
- F and O: estimated at 3,000 and 1,500. If O were 3,000 (writing-heavy), Haiku's tiered output rate would raise the continuing side more than the fresh side. Fresh still wins by class.
- B and R: B measured. R was given as 15,000. If R were 25,000, fresh would rise by about 0.06 USD. Continue stays cheaper.
- M: 0, since no review pauses are expected. Three misses would add about 0.28 USD to continue (Haiku long-context rate) and about 1.22 USD to fresh (Sonnet cache-write rate). Fresh gets more expensive, so the cost gap grows, and the class rule still decides.
- Task type: a refactor within one module. If it hid a cross-module design choice, the target would be Opus and the split would be priced. Fresh would still win by class.
- Prices: 1 day old. The long-context tier is applied from turn 2. Without the tier, continue is 0.09 USD and the class rule still decides.
- Unrecorded context: none. If five turns of rediscovery were needed (L 5), fresh would rise by about 0.22 USD (5 times C_fresh * 0.1, G * 2.5 and O * 10, at Sonnet rates). Fresh still wins by class.
