### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: fresh. Continuing averages 367,500 tokens of context over 30 turns, against 107,500 for a fresh session, a ratio of about 3.4. The stage is a Sonnet-class refactor, so if gpt-6.1-sol sits below the Sonnet class, rule 1 gives fresh by class. A handoff of 2,500 tokens buys a fresh start that reads 15,000. With the Sonnet row's ratios (output 100 times cache_read, cache_write 25 times cache_read), T* would be about 4.8 turns, so 30 turns would repay the handoff. Those ratios come from a different row and are not a figure for gpt-6.1-sol, so this does not change the verdict.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- B and R: B at 50,000 raises C_fresh to 132,500. Verdict direction unchanged.
- M (review pauses): none expected. Each miss re-writes the whole context at cache-write rate, which favours fresh, so zero misses is the case least favourable to fresh.
- Task type: the situation says a refactor within one module. If it turns out to need cross-module changes, the class moves to Opus and fresh wins by class.
- Prices: unknown. No adverse value can be computed.
