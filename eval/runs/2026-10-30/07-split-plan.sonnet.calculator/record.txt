### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continue in this session. It holds all the context, needs no brief, has no cold start, and the context stays far under K (575,000 against 970,000), so there is no compaction risk. A brief is work, and a cheaper session might misread the contract.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured. If it is 30 percent lower (245,000), continue gets cheaper but stays dearer than a Sonnet run, because the fable rates are 2.5 to 5 times higher.
- T: a plan estimate. If it doubles to 100, see the recomputation below.
- F and O: unmeasured defaults. Higher F and O raise every side; the rate gap remains, so the order holds.
- B and R: B measured. R covers the design note, handoff and modules; if the modules are larger, run and fresh costs rise a little. A larger R barely moves the gap to continue.
- M (review pauses): none expected. If the operator reviews often, misses hit the larger context harder, which favours the smaller-context fresh sessions. Tested with M = 10 below.
- Task type: the risk is that the stage hides a design choice. The design is finished and the contract, tests and done definition are stated, so the brief can carry it. If the Sonnet run found the contract wrong, the escalation line returns it to a higher class; acceptance by Opus catches an error that slips through.
- Prices: table 1 day old, no tier in play for these models. The Sonnet and Opus cache_write figures are derived as 1.25 times input (table note).
- Unrecorded context: none. If something were unrecorded, L turns would add to fresh and split, so the brief must be checked to hold everything.
