### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continue. This session already holds a green understanding of the patch, a handoff is work, and a fresh session starts colder. A release push is the kind of step where a stronger class (sonnet, not haiku) is safer, and a fresh sonnet session would not need a downgrade. The cost gap is small in absolute terms (0.72 USD).

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured. If 30 percent lower (280,000), C_cont = 302,500 and continue costs 0.4538 + 0.1125 + 0.15 = 0.716 USD; fresh is still far cheaper.
- T: if it doubles to 30, see the recomputation below.
- F and O (and the effort O assumes): measured at medium; high effort may raise O. If O doubles to 2,000 the growth is 4,000 and both sides rise, fresh by the tier output (2.5 against 10 per million for continue), so the gap widens, not narrows. Marked as an estimate.
- B and R: B measured; R is the handoff and patch only, as the situation states. If R is 30 percent larger (10,400), B + R = 96,850 and the tier starts one turn earlier; the cost moves by about 0.01 USD.
- M (review pauses): 0 by default. One pause at the approval step is expected in practice; a miss costs about 0.047 USD on continue (422,500 * 2.4/1e6 = 1.01 USD actually; see note) against 117,000 * 0.575/1e6 = 0.067 USD on fresh (tier). A miss favours fresh more, as step 2 says. Verdict direction unchanged. Note: continue miss = 422,500 * (2.5 - 0.1)/1e6 = 1.014 USD.
- Task type: mechanical and fully specified; the only judgement is the push, which is covered by the approval step. No hidden design choice named in the situation.
- Prices: table 1 day old; the haiku tier was applied turn by turn from turn 3. No null.
- Unrecorded context: none; everything is on disk, so L = 0 and no loss penalty.
