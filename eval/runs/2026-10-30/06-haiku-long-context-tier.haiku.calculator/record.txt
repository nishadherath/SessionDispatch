### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: a fresh Haiku session would start with a clean 96,450-token context built from a 10,000-token brief, not from a 90,000-token session that carries prior work the rename does not need. The mid-stage growth would be the same, and the fresh session would avoid carrying whatever the current context holds. Fresh would also keep the working set small for review. The cost gap is only 0.03 USD, and a clean context is a quality gain that the record does not price.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured at 90,000. If it were 30 percent higher (117,000), the continue side rises and the verdict moves to a near tie (see the recomputation below).
- T: 20 plan estimate. If it doubled to 40, the continue advantage grows, because the reads difference is fixed per turn at about 6,450 tokens times the cache-read rate, and the fixed fresh costs (the handoff and first read) are spread over more turns.
- F and O: defaults of 3,000 and 1,500, unmeasured. O at medium may be higher for 40 edits. Output adds equally to both sides, so it moves the margin little.
- B and R: B is measured. R is 10,000 from the situation. A 20,000 re-read would raise the fresh side.
- M (review pauses): none expected. A review pause longer than the cache lifetime would add misses on both sides, the larger on the continue side's bigger context, so it favours fresh a little. Not counted: the situation states no pauses.
- Task type: a mechanical rename across docs with a check script. If the work hides a design choice (which name to use where), the matrix row would change to Sonnet or Opus. The 40 edits are stated as reviewed, so the row holds.
- Prices: the table is 1 day old and the haiku row is complete. The long-context tier is applied turn by turn.
- Unrecorded context: stated as none. If some was missing, L turns of rediscovery would add to the fresh side only, which favours continue more.
