### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continue is the reversible default, there is no handoff to write, and no cold start. Fresh also drops a risk that the brief misses something. But continuing runs at fable prices (cache write 12.5 against 2.5 for sonnet) and costs 12.34 USD against 4.77, and a context of 350,000 tokens drags into every turn. Everything the stage needs is on disk, so little is lost.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured. At 30 percent lower (245,000) continue falls to about 9.3 USD, still well above split.
- T: 50 is an estimate with no range. At 100 (hand computed): continue 27.50, fresh 12.79, split 8.36 USD (split 53 percent under fresh). At 25: fresh 2.88, split 3.40 USD, so fresh beats split. The split-versus-fresh crossover is at about 35 turns.
- F and O: defaults. At high effort O could be higher; doubling O to 3,000 raises every side and does not reorder them.
- B and R: B measured; R stated. Raising R raises fresh and split alike.
- M: 0 unmeasured, no pauses expected. Misses would favour the fresh sides, so they would widen the gap.
- Task type: design-heavy by definition. The risk is that the brief hides an open design choice; the design note states every contract, so no.
- Prices: table 1 day old; no long-context tier applies to these rows.
- Unrecorded context: none. If some existed, L turns would add to the fresh and split sides.

Recomputed with the adverse value that moves the result most (T, between 25 and 100):

```

T = 25:  cost_fresh = 2.88, cost_split = 3.40
```

Verdict holds: yes, fragile: the split loses to fresh by cost at a T under about 35. Continue loses to both across the whole range. The fresh side also omits two small terms (2 to 11 percent of the smaller side); the split's 17.8 percent margin is outside that range. No T range was given, so the stress value does not change the verdict.
