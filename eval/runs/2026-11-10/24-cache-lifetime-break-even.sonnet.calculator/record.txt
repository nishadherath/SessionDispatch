### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: fresh is the cheaper side as soon as T passes 14.2, and the session carries 400,000 tokens of context that this stage does not need. Every turn reads 419,500 tokens at cache-read rates against 119,500 in a fresh session. The stage has been measured on only three fixes, so 13 turns could be 15 or 16. Everything needed is on disk, so a fresh session loses nothing.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured, 400,000. If 30 percent lower (280,000), the gap (C0 - B - R) falls to 180,000 and T* rises to about 23.6, so continue gets stronger. If 30 percent higher (520,000), T* falls to about 10.7 and fresh wins.
- T: 13 from three measured fixes. T* is 14.2, so T of 15 or more flips to fresh (T 26 gives fresh 1.36 against continue 1.71 USD, 26.1 percent).
- F and O: measured at medium effort. Larger F or O raise G and the write term on both sides equally; they leave T* nearly unchanged.
- B and R: B measured, R stated. A bigger R raises the fresh first read (13,550 tokens at 4.00/M is 0.054 USD) and pushes T* out, favouring continue.
- M (review pauses): none expected. One miss would add 1.14 USD to continue's side and flip to fresh (continue 2.47, fresh 1.33 USD). It is not claimed: no pauses are expected, and under 1h few pauses miss.
- Task type: row 2, one module, with a test oracle; nothing hides a design choice.
- Prices: table 1 day old; 1h write price used, as measured. At the 5-minute write price (2.5) the first read would cost 0.25 and T* would fall, but the measured lifetime is 1h.
- Unrecorded context: none, so L is 0. Any item found would add rediscovery turns to the fresh side only.
