### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continuing avoids writing and reading a handoff and keeps the session's warm cache and its unwritten sense of the code. The 0.15 USD margin is only 11.7 percent, and the omitted terms (the handoff turn's own read, the new session's turns spent reading R) are worth 2 to 11 percent of the smaller side, so the real margin could be nearly nil. The compaction penalty here is only 0.075 USD, and everything the next stage needs is already on disk, so little is lost by staying.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: estimated from characters, not measured. At 30 percent lower (80,500) the session still crosses K (C_end 215,500), so the crossing holds.
- T: a plan estimate. At 15 the context still just crosses K (182,500). At 14 or fewer it does not cross, and continue wins.
- F and O: defaults at medium effort. A refactor could read more files per turn, which raises growth and favours fresh through the compaction.
- B and R: B is the 25,000 default and unmeasured; R is a character count. A lower B favours fresh, a higher one favours continue.
- M (review pauses): 0, none expected. The 5-minute lifetime would make any pause over 5 minutes a miss on the larger continue context, which favours fresh.
- Task type: row 2 stands; a refactor within one module with its tests as the oracle hides no design choice.
- Prices: table 1 day old, no long-context tier on the sonnet row, 5-minute write price used because the harness documents that lifetime.
- Unrecorded context: none; all of it is on disk. If some were found, L above 0 would favour continue.
