### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: a fresh session starts at 146,450 average context against 225,000, and it would drop 94,000 tokens of exploration history that the next stage does not need, because everything it needs is on disk. A smaller context also lowers the risk of drift in a stage of 20 turns. The handoff is only about 80 lines and costs 0.025 USD to write.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured (180,000). At 30 percent lower (126,000) the margin for continue grows, since T* rises as C0 - B - R shrinks; continue stays cheaper (1.91 against 2.09 USD at T 40 in a trial run).
- T: a plan estimate. At T doubled to 40 the hand-computed figures are continue 2.13 and fresh 2.10 USD. Fresh is cheaper by 0.035 USD (1.7 percent), inside the 10 percent band.
- F and O (medium effort): defaults, unmeasured. Heavier F or O raise both sides almost equally; the gap is C0 minus B minus R, a fixed 78,550 tokens, so they move T* only through the divisor and not the verdict.
- B and R: B is measured. R of 15,000 could be larger if the module is big; a larger R lowers the fresh side's gain from a smaller context, favouring continue further.
- M (review pauses): none expected. One miss under a 5-minute cache costs the larger context more (about 0.04 USD more on continue than on fresh), a small push toward fresh that does not reach the 10 percent line.
- Task type: row 2 with a stated test suite; no hidden design choice, since the situation gives one feature in one module.
- Prices: table 1 day old, sonnet row has no long-context tier, ttl 5m is measured.
- Unrecorded context: none, so L is 0. If L were 5, the fresh side would rise by about 0.1 USD, favouring continue.
