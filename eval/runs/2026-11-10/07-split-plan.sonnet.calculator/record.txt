### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: fresh in one opus session (5.62 USD) avoids a second handoff and a second session start for the operator, and keeps one session holding the whole cross-module picture. Cross-module work is row 3 opus work, and handing the run to sonnet risks a weaker result that acceptance then rejects, costing a rerun. Continuing on fable (12.34 USD) keeps all context but pays more than twice the fresh price for work that does not need fable.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured. At 30 percent lower (245,000) continue is cheaper but still above both fresh plans; the verdict is not affected because fresh and split do not depend on C0.
- T: 50 is an estimate. At 100, the split gets relatively cheaper (hand-computed: fresh about 12.79 USD, split about 8.36 USD, 35 percent cheaper). At 25, fresh is cheaper (about 2.88 against 3.40 USD, split 18 percent dearer). Fragile: the split stops winning by more than 10 percent below T of about 35 turns (linear estimate between the two points).
- F and O: defaults, unmeasured. A larger O raises every side; the split's sonnet run is the cheapest per output token, so higher O favours the split.
- B and R: B measured; R from the situation. If R is larger the fresh and run sides rise together; the margin is little affected.
- M (review pauses): none expected. Any miss costs more on the opus context than the sonnet one at the same size, favouring the split slightly.
- Task type: the real risk. If the stage hides design choices, sonnet at medium may fail; the design note states every contract and the 14 tests, which is why the run is row 2. Opus acceptance at high is the guard.
- Prices: table 1 day old; no long-context tier on the rows used.
- Unrecorded context: none, so L is 0; the brief does not retell this session.
