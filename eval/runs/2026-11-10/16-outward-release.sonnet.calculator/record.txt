### Adversarial pass

The opposite verdict, argued: continue. The session holds the full context, there is no handoff to write, no cold start, and the whole stage is only 15 turns with the files on disk anyway. Continuing avoids one operator click and any handoff mistake, and a haiku session near its long-context threshold loses part of its price advantage. A 60-line handoff may also hide an assumption.

Each input, the way it could be wrong:

- C0: measured 400,000. If 30 percent lower (280,000), continue is cheaper (reads 15*(280,000+22,500)*0.1/M = 0.454, total about 0.72) but still above fresh 0.18.
- T: if 30 turns, continue about 1.9, fresh about 0.5; fresh holds.
- F and O (and the effort O assumes): O measured at an unknown effort; high effort could double O. Tested below.
- B and R: B measured. R is only the handoff and patch; if it omitted the project instruction files, they are already inside B.
- M (review pauses): none expected; a pause longer than 5 minutes at the approval gate would add one miss (about 117,000 * (0.625 - 0.05)/M = 0.07 on the fresh side, about 0.99 on the continue side), favouring fresh further. The approval wait is outside the stage.
- Task type: it is mechanical as stated (reviewed patch plus check script). If the patch needed judgment, the sonnet class would be needed, see below.
- Prices: table 1 day old. The haiku tier is applied from turn 3 on.
- Unrecorded context: none claimed, L = 0; the situation says all is on disk.
